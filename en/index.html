What am I looking at?
  • The gradient ∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y) points in the direction of steepest ascent and is perpendicular to the level curves.
  • The algorithm moves in the opposite direction: xk+1 = xk − α ∇f(xk).
  • With a large α the method oscillates or diverges; with a small α it converges slowly.
  • It stops when ‖∇f‖ < tolerance: a critical point (minimum, maximum or saddle).
  • The plane z = c cuts the surface along the level curve f(x, y) = c.

The function accepts ** or ^, sin cos tan exp log sqrt abs, pi, e. With the simulator focused: Space start/pause · → one step · R reset.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What kind of content can you create?

Explanatory animations with Manim, interactive simulators that run in the browser, and LaTeX course materials: exams, problem sets and summaries with TikZ figures.

What topics do you cover?

University-level single-variable and multivariable calculus, algebra and physics, especially vector calculus, optimization and electromagnetism.

What tools do you work with?

Python with Manim for the animations, JavaScript with Plotly.js and math.js for the simulators, and LaTeX with TikZ and pgfplots for the documents.

Do the simulators need to be installed?

No. They run in the browser, on phones too, and can be embedded in a course platform or published as a standalone page.

Can I see the code?

Yes, it's all on GitHub: the Manim scenes, the simulator and the scripts I use to check the math.

How is the content checked?

The math is checked before animating. In the trickiest scenes, a separate script verifies it numerically, as in the logarithm one.
